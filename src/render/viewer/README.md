# src/render/viewer and src/render/skins: the PixiJS half (WP-M2D)

**Owner:** WP-M2D (`viewer/**` except `skin.ts`, and `skins/**`). The contract files (`viewer/skin.ts`, `../types.ts`, `../index.ts`, `../README.md`) are the architect's. Design: [`05-technical-m2.md`](../../../docs/design/_drafts/05-technical-m2.md) §A5, §A9, §A10, §A18; art direction 04 §3, §4, §7, §9, §10 (asset-006); the UI designer's specification: [`docs/design/mockups/arena-notes.md`](../../../docs/design/mockups/arena-notes.md) §1 and `src/ui/styles/components.css` §25 (the `.atoken`, `.abar`, `.apip`, `.atele`, `.aimpact`, `.anum`, `.acallout` rules are the geometry the viewer draws).

`createArenaViewer(options)` plays one round's full-level `FightLog` against a playback clock. It never simulates, draws randomness or writes game state. Each frame it samples `Timeline.sampleAt` at the clock's tick and draws the `ArenaFrameState`; the HUD is DOM (`src/ui`), fed by `onFrame` at `hudHz`.

## Layer order (bottom to top)
floor and wall band (skin) · row markers (faint dashed lines; labels only at kickoff) · **ground marks: zones and telegraphs, clipped to the field rectangle** · low cues · **bodies with their token marks, depth-sorted by screen y** · projectiles · high cues (impacts, swings, rings) · overlay (bars, pips, badges: never hidden by a body) · **top cues (floating numbers, Evade/Resisted words, the down callouts), above the overlays so a neighbour's stack never hides a number** · overtime edge.

The viewer translates body containers and **never rotates, scales or mirrors them**; a body picks its direction or frame from `UnitRenderState.facing`. Only the viewer's own wedge turns. Everything with game meaning is drawn by the viewer above the skin: the side ring (ours solid, theirs double) with plate keylines, the wedge, the **role-glyph plate** and glyph(s) (drawn above the body, so a club colour never hides a glyph), the hit wash, the selection brackets, bars, pips, badge, crown, labels, telegraphs and cues.

## The palette is the only source (A18)
Every colour, and every alpha, size and duration that has a token, comes from `ArenaPalette` and `palette.metrics` (ring width/line/gap, down opacity, zone and telegraph alphas, hit-flash alpha and ms, flash limit, number merge window and rise, label, pip and bar sizes). The metrics are CSS px at any zoom (the host measured the rem sizes, so `--ui-scale` reaches the canvas). A burst the flash limiter refused is drawn at `metrics.zoneAlpha` (a ghost); the cast-bar fill uses `metrics.telegraphProgressAlpha`.

**What is still a number in code** (design constants taken from `components.css` §25 or A18, named where they are used; they have no token): the designer's proportions of the disc (wedge 0.2 × 0.26 m, crown 0.42 m; the plate radii, glyph sizes and bar width are tokens now: `metrics.plateRadiusM`, `plateRadiusHybridM`, `glyphSizeM`, `glyphSizeHybridM`, `barWidthM`, in metres of arena), stroke widths (the 1 px keylines and hairlines, the 2 px telegraph and halo strokes, the 6 px band of a double telegraph edge), the 0.55 zone-edge alpha, and the 360 ms overtime fade (A18 decision 6). `design.test.ts` scans the source for **colour** literals (`0xRRGGBB`, `#` hex in 3, 4, 6 or 8 digits, `rgb()`/`hsl()`-style functions, named colours); the only exceptions are the white of tinted masks (`0xffffff`, `'white'` in `glyphs.ts`) and `StatusCue.ring`'s state name `'grey'`.

A host re-reads the tokens and calls `setPalette` on a theme, motion or interface-scale change: the viewer re-bakes the number fonts, redraws every token and the floor. Tests, the harness and a host's first frame use WP-M2C's `defaultArenaPalette(theme)` / `DEFAULT_ARENA_PALETTES` (`src/render/colors`).
- **Body colours are exact club colours** (exempt from 3:1, A18 decision 4). Rings, the plate, bars and pip outlines keep 3:1.
- **Text** sits on plate chips in `ink` (labels, tag, name, rule badge, cast bar, "+n", callouts); floating numbers have no chip and a plate-coloured stroke. Numbers always carry a sign (U+2212 for damage, + for heal and shield).
- **The stack stays in the host.** The stack above a token (bars, row, cast bar, tag, name) is drawn below the disc, in the same order outwards and with the bar block mirrored so the focus bar stays nearest, when above would leave the host (the north wall); it is nudged sideways when it would cross the west or east edge (2 px margin). If neither fits, it stays above.
- **Floating numbers** sit beside the disc (right, or left when the right would leave the host), at its middle height, on the top layer, so they are never over the token's own stack. **Down callouts** ("X downed by Y · Ability") queue along one end of the field (its top or bottom edge, centred), the end with fewer bodies near the centre line (chosen when the queue opens and kept while it is open), one line each; a captain going down is **one line** ("Captain X downed by ..."), the captain-down cue folds into the down cue and a lone captain-down keeps its own line. A ring burst marks where the unit fell. The callout always carries the fallen, the credited fighter and the ability (an ability the content does not know is left out, never shown as its id) for its whole 2 s life: `callout-real.test.ts` checks every down of the committed real rounds, replayed and live. The canvas text is not in the DOM; the smoke's vis-003 check reads only the DOM (the feed, where the ability is on a second line). A cast's name is written once, on the cast bar (the telegraph has no name chip).
- **Damage kinds differ by shape:** physical = angular spark, arcane = round burst, true = diamond.
- **Status pips** come from `StatusCue.pip` (null for the shield category: its bar segment shows it): a chip outlined in the family colour with the family silhouette from `palette.pipGlyphSvg`; hard control first, "+n" chip for the rest. Hard control greys the ring and keeps its style.
- **Overtime:** a static thin `warn` line on the inner edge of the wall band (the field edge), fading in over 360 ms (instant under reduced motion, never pulsing); no horn.
- **Downed bodies** stay (flattened to 60% at once, down opacity, a diamond in the side colour, bars and wedge gone) until the next `load` (the next kickoff).

## Rules the code keeps
- **No future leaks (review B1).** A live round never seeks past `headTick`. `endTick` is null until playback reaches the end. `keyMoments()` is asked up to the head; auto-pause stops are looked up only to the end of the frame being drawn. A stop fires when playback crosses it going forward (never when seeking, never again for the tick it resumes from). After a rewind, a live round stops at its head (`{ kind: 'head' }`).
- **A live round is drawn one frame interval behind its head.** The head (the reveal frontier, the cursor, `headTick`, every bounded query) runs ahead of the drawn tick by at most `header.frameEvery` (2 ticks); each live sample passes `knownUntilTick` = the head, so interpolating toward the next frame stays inside what is known and motion is as smooth as a replay's (drawing AT the head stepped positions at the 10 Hz frame rate). What the UI receives is bounded by the head as before. At a stop (an auto-pause, the head after a rewind, the end) the display catches up: it shows the stop tick S, `headTick` is S, `onFrame` gets the state at S and `onStop` carries S. At the end the display catches up to the end tick. A rewound live round (more than a frame interval behind its head) replays shown ticks and stops at the head.
- **One stop per boundary (A7, ux-001).** Stops that land on the same tick resolve as the strongest one: the end, then an auto-pause, then the head. A deciding down that auto-pauses on the round's end tick is reported as the end only (`{ kind: 'end', tick }`), so the player presses Play once to leave the round, not once for the pause and again for the end. The end stop carries no cause; the UI can read the down from `keyMoments()` at the end tick for the round-end card. `end-stop.test.ts` checks it on the real model, replayed and live.
- **State is a function of (log, tick).** Seeking and playing show the same picture. Presentation-only effects (flash admission, number merging) use wall time and never feed back.
- **Flashes.** Per fighter, flash starts are at least 1000/limit ms apart in wall time (`metrics.flashLimitPerSecond`, 3 by default), so 4x cannot raise the rate. The token's hit wash lasts `metrics.hitFlashMs` and a refused hit shows none; burst effects draw dimmed. None under reduced motion or while paused/seeking. No full-screen flash exists.
- **Numbers.** At most one per fighter per `metrics.numberMergeMs` of wall time; later ones add into the first.
- **Reduced motion.** `sampleAt` drops the motion-only effects and zeroes the badge pulse; the scene also never draws those kinds, pins pulses and growth to their settled look, shows no trails, no camera ease, no body easing. Telegraph progress stays.
- **Objects.** Pooled by unit index and effect id (`KeyedPool`); static geometry is redrawn only when zoom or palette change.
- **Teardown.** Everything is destroyed with `DESTROY_ALL` (`{ children: true, context: true }`). PixiJS v8 destroys a Graphics' own context only when called with no options or `context: true`; plain `{ children: true }` leaked about 0.65 MB per round (found by the 20-round check). `destroy()` also loses the WebGL context. **Warning-free (final QA D4):** `destroy()` stops the ticker first and a tick in progress (an `onStop` callback that leaves the arena) ends without rendering; the stage lets go of the scene; the scene and its fonts go before the renderer. Fonts are released with `uninstallFont` (`scene/text.ts`): a label of 50 or more glyphs (a long callout or name) is too big to batch, so PixiJS draws it as a graphic of its own and caches its texture bind group for good, listening to the font page; destroying the page then logged "[BindGroup] a 'textureSource' / 'textureSampler' was destroyed while still bound to a shader" (one pair per font, only when such a label had been drawn: about a third of exits). `uninstallFont` drops those stale listeners first. Checked by `scene/text.test.ts` (PixiJS's own classes) and `index.test.ts` (destroy inside a tick, safe order, a console.warn spy over play, skip, destroy), and in a real browser with 50+ glyph callouts (no warnings after the fix, one pair before).
- **Keyboard (A18 decision 3).** The canvas is one tab stop (`tabindex 0`, `role="img"`, a label rewritten at each pause, seek and stop, never at the HUD rate while playing) that never cycles internally; Tab selection moves through the fighter strip's cards, and a click on a token reports `onSelect` so the UI can focus its card. The viewer sets no focus outline: style `:focus-visible` on the canvas (or `:has(canvas:focus-visible)`) outside the wall.

## Options and the model contract (A19)
`ArenaViewerOptions` extends `ArenaViewerOptionExtras` and `ArenaViewer` extends `ArenaViewerDecisionHistory` (both in `src/render/types.ts`): `cues` (default `buildCueTable(content)`), `viewTransform` (default the real camera), `tabStop` (default true; false when the host element is the tab stop), and `decisionsUpTo(unit, tick?)`: the unit's committed decisions from round start, oldest first, up to `tick` (default the current tick) and never past `headTick`, each the shape `decisionAt` returns (count by `rule` for "rules fired so far"). `flashLimitPerSecond` defaults to `palette.metrics.flashLimitPerSecond`.
- **Live samples carry `knownUntilTick`** = the live head (the larger of the head and the tick this frame shows), so interpolated positions, effect anchors and projectile starts never use a frame past it; a replay passes none (`real-model.test.ts` compares the viewer's picture with a log truncated at the head).
- **Reduced motion and `age`:** the model gives non-number effects `age` 1 meaning "drawn static at the end state", so they are not faded by `1 - age` in that mode; numbers fade in place.

## Checks and measurements
- **Logic (happy-dom, no GPU):** `npm test` runs the clock, core, scene, design and skin tests; they use `testing/fake-timeline.ts` and a real `ArenaScene` over scene-graph objects.
- **Real model, no GPU:** `real-model.test.ts` runs the viewer on WP-M2C's real timeline, cues, camera and colours over committed simulator rounds (`testing/real-fixtures.ts`).
- **Real browser:** serve the repo with `npx vite --port 5299 --strictPort` and open `/src/render/viewer/dev/harness.html` (`?theme=light&reduced=1&speed=4&play=1&tick=320&labels=all&select=2&fixture=a`; `?fake=1` for the scripted fake timeline). It runs the REAL timeline, cue table, camera and content over a committed simulator log (`dev/real.ts`). `window.__harness` exposes `capture()`, `liveCanvasDataUrl()` (what the F8 screenshot reads), `measure(ms, speed)`, `rounds(n, playMs)`, `cycle(n)`, `failures()`, `ariaWrites(ms)` and `setStopHook(fn)`.
- **Measured here** (the harness on the REAL timeline, cues, camera and content, over a committed simulator round; headless Edge 156 over CDP):
  - WebGL capture works: `snapshot()` and `canvas.toDataURL()` are both non-blank, also at DPR 2.
  - Frame rate, 1920 × 1080, RTX 5060 (hardware D3D11): median interval 17.9 ms, p95 18.5 ms at 1x and 18.4 ms at 4x (the harness paces at about 56 fps; the Claude pane's vsync gave 60 fps and p95 17.1 ms on the fake timeline). Our CPU per frame p50 0.3 ms, p95 0.4 to 0.5 ms. SwiftShader (software GL) gives about 9.6 fps with our CPU p95 at 1.1 ms: it measures the rasteriser, not us.
  - Heap after GC: +0.7 MB over 18 further round loads (38.6 to 39.3 MB), +1.5 MB over 20 create/destroy cycles; no canvas left behind, no WebGL context warning; failed creations (skin load, cue table, no WebGL) leave nothing.
  - Chunks, an app-like build (`src/main.ts` plus a lazy `import()` of the viewer): viewer-containing chunks 192.2 KB gzip (PixiJS about 148, the viewer with the real timeline, cues and colours 43.8), startup JS 212.1 KB, no viewer or PixiJS module in the startup chunks.
  - Not the reference machine (Q35): the 60 fps criterion is measured by WP-M2F on the smoke build.

## Known gaps
- Number fonts are baked per tone at the renderer's resolution at creation (and re-baked on a palette change); moving to a higher-DPR monitor keeps the old glyph resolution until the next viewer.
- `drawCalls` in `stats()` is null (PixiJS v8 exposes no counter).
- Oblique projection draws circles as ellipses for ground marks; effects stay screen-space.
- An effect's lifetime is the model's (`EFFECT_LIFETIME_TICKS`); `metrics.fxFadeMs` is not applied by the viewer (the model owns lifetimes; numbers fade by their age).
